Wren moment

Flood a money, and it turns into bogwool.

In Ulm Hollow, bogwool was too bulky to be money. Cowries got there the other way: by 1890, one silver dollar's worth of shells weighed about as much as a child (20,000 shells at 1–2 g each: 20–40 kg). In 1904 the British banned new shell imports in Southern Nigeria, and coins slowly took over.

Wren found a cave. Hamburg found a reef. Nobody broke a rule: everyone in the chain just bought cheap shells. The people who lost were the ones holding shells as savings.

In real life: when lots of new money is made, prices don't jump for everyone on the same day. The saver with a jar (or a bank account) finds out last.
